The class and I had many discussions about where AI is and where it is going, especially in software engineering and the future of that role. On a few points, I think some of the class thought I had lost my mind. Driving home, I listened to a random YouTube talk and, interestingly, the speaker Gergely Orosz seemed to echo many of my thoughts, but he communicated them a lot better. I would highly recommend this talk if you work anywhere near software.

Some of the points I really liked:

Titus Winterss (co-author of Software Engineering at Google) frames success as intelligence, wisdom and charisma. AI makes intelligence (knowing how) cheap, so wisdom (knowing what) and charisma (convincing others) matter more.

Much of what Gergely Orosz highlighted as coming next is exactly what I highlighted to my own students:   - Agents running in the cloud on VMs. Running locally is so early 2026.   - No longer reading the code. A matter of when, not if.   - Evolving internal infrastructure to reduce risk and further accelerate the rate of change. Evals become part of CI/CD, and agents take part in deployments, observability and incident response.

One quote that particularly stuck with me:

“Everyone is playing the theater of doing reviews, but with the volume of changes that get thrown your way… people just find the path of least resistance. Some teammates just give up and they issue ‘looks good to me’ on all of them.” Gergely Orosz
